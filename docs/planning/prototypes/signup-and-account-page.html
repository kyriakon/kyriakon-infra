Secure encrypted email

Your mail is encrypted to your own key the moment it arrives. The server holds ciphertext and no key, so we cannot read your messages, and no request or order on our side changes that. The cost is that if you lose your key and your recovery phrase, the mail is gone: nobody here can restore it. Keep the two in different places.

A website and a capsule

Your own site at yourname.kyriakon.net, uploaded over sftp on port 22. A gemini capsule at the same name if you want one. Your `pass` repositories over ssh on port 2222.

Example of the other two answers:

Your mail would be unreadable. This key asks for an AEAD cipher (packet tag 34). GnuPG will use it when we encrypt to you and some mail clients cannot open the result. Ask your client to remove AEAD from the key's preferred ciphers, or make a new key with the generator on the application page.
There is no encryption subkey. This key can sign but cannot be encrypted to, so mail to you would be refused at our end rather than delivered. Make a key with an encryption subkey.
This key has expired, or its encryption subkey has. A key that has run out cannot receive mail, and a subkey can run out while the rest of the key still looks usable. Either extend it in your mail program, or make a new key with the generator and tell us the new one.

Keys

Both keys are made here, in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere until you copy the public parts into the application.

1. Your mail key

This is the key your mail is encrypted to. Two things happen when you press the button:

  • A key pair is made on your computer, using an algorithm your mail client supports.
  • You are given a recovery phrase of eight words from a 2,048-word list, 88 bits. Write it down, on paper, now.

Your recovery phrase, write it down:

harbour lantern thistle quarry almond vesper ridge marrow

Then download your key file:

The file is locked with that phrase. You need both, and you need them apart: the file on a disk or in your backups, the phrase on paper. Anyone who has both can read your mail. We have neither, which is why we cannot read it, and why nobody here can recover it for you.

2. Your upload key

This is what lets you put a website up and push to your repositories. It is not the mail key and it does not decrypt anything. Pressing this gives you a private key file and a public key to paste.

Keep the private file somewhere safe: it is the only copy. We never see it, and the public half is all we get.

# Taking an Indian OIDAR registration

**Question:** the decision behind ticket 249 makes the UK sole trader take the Indian GST
registration for online information and database access or retrieval services only when a card
sale's billing country is India. What does FORM GST REG-10 ask of a supplier with no place of
business in India, what does the registration cost and how long does it take, is there a lead
time before the first supply or a period allowed for registering after it, what rate does the
return apply, and does the simplified scheme oblige the supplier to appoint a person in India?

**Answer:** registering after the first Indian sale is on time, and the rest is short.

1. **The registration is takeable after the first sale.** Rule 10(2) of the Central Goods and
   Services Tax Rules gives a registration applied for within thirty days of the date the person
   becomes liable an effective date of that earlier date, and the GST portal's own OIDAR FAQ
   states the same thirty-day rule for this registration. A later application is effective only
   from the date it is granted.
2. **A UK sole trader supplies an Indian authorised signatory rather than appointing a person in
   India.** The portal's FAQ makes an Indian authorised signatory with a valid PAN a
   pre-requisite for the application. A representative in India is a separate and optional
   appointment, and IGST section 14(2) permits it rather than requiring it.
3. **The rules prescribe no fee, and the general registration is decided within seven working
   days.** The portal's OIDAR material has no payment step and states no service level of its
   own; the officer handling this registration sits at Bengaluru West.
4. **The rate is 18 per cent,** on the CBIC's own rate schedule for services, and a nil FORM
   GSTR-5A is due in a month with no Indian sales because rule 64 states no nil exemption.

## 1. What FORM GST REG-10 asks

[Rule 14(1)](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/rules/cgst_rules/active/chapter3/rule14_v1.00.html)
of the CGST Rules requires the supplier to submit an application, signed or verified through an
electronic verification code, in FORM GST REG-10 at the common portal, directly or through a
facilitation centre. On the common portal this is the Non-Resident Online Services Provider
registration, and the GST portal publishes a step-by-step manual and a question-and-answer page
for it.

The [manual](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/registration/Non-Resident_Online_Services_Provider_manual.htm)
lists the Part A fields: the legal name of the person, the Permanent Account Number of the
person, the tax identification number or unique number of the country of origin, the name of an
authorised signatory who belongs to India as per the PAN database, the signatory's PAN, the
signatory's email address and Indian mobile number, and, if a representative is appointed in
India, the representative's name, PAN, email address and mobile number. Part A generates a
temporary reference number, and Part B has to be completed within fifteen days of it or the
reference number and the data entered against it are purged. The manual marks the PAN of the
person as optional and says a person without one can apply for PAN, and it marks the signatory's
mobile number as optional.

The document list comes from the [FAQ](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/registration/Non-Resident_Online_Services_Provider.htm).

| Group | Documents, all PDF or JPG up to 100 KB |
|---|---|
| Proof of appointment of the authorised signatory | Photograph of the signatory (JPG only), letter of authorisation, copy of a resolution passed by the board of directors or managing committee with an acceptance letter |
| Proof of bank details | First page of a passbook, cancelled cheque, bank statement |
| Proof of the supplier | Clearance certificate issued by the Government of India, certificate of incorporation from India or abroad, licence if issued by the origin country |

The portal makes bank details optional for an OIDAR applicant from 27 December 2018, and says they
are needed only if an Indian authorised representative is nominated
([manual](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/registration/Non-Resident_Online_Services_Provider_manual.htm),
FAQ 17).

**Whether an authorised person or agent must be named.** The FAQ states that a non-resident
OIDAR supplier "need to appoint an Authorised Signatory (Indian) in India possessing a valid
PAN", and that this signatory applies to the portal on the supplier's behalf (FAQ 4). A
representative in India is a separate, optional appointment: Part A asks "Do you have a
representative appointed in India", and where the answer is yes the representative's name, PAN,
email address and mobile number are mandatory (FAQ 18). The Act draws the same line, and its
words are quoted in section 5.

**Two statements that conflict.** The manual says the authorised signatory "must be a resident
of India with a valid PAN Card", while the same manual marks the signatory's PAN field optional
and FAQ 15 says the signatory's PAN "is not mandatory". The pre-requisite in FAQ 4 is the
stronger statement, so the safe reading is that an Indian signatory with a valid PAN is needed
*(interpretation)*. The supplier's own PAN is optional in both sources.

**One gap for a sole trader.** The proof of the supplier is one of three corporate documents
(clearance certificate, certificate of incorporation, or an origin-country licence). A sole
trader has no certificate of incorporation, and the portal's material does not say whether an
origin-country licence covers a sole trader or what a sole trader with neither document uploads.
This is unresolved in the sources reached.

## 2. What the registration costs and how long it takes

No fee appears anywhere in the process. [Rule 14](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/rules/cgst_rules/active/chapter3/rule14_v1.00.html)
sets out the application and the certificate, [rule 9](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/rules/cgst_rules/active/chapter3/rule9_v1.00.html)
sets out the verification, and neither states a charge, and the portal's OIDAR manual has no
payment step, ending at the acknowledgement of the application. No primary source reached states
in words that the registration is free, so the finding is that the rules and the portal
prescribe no fee and the cost is whatever an accountant or agent charges *(interpretation)*.

Rule 9(1) gives the general time: the proper officer examines the application and, if it is in
order, approves registration "within a period of seven working days from the date of submission
of the application". The current text says seven working days; the CBIC's 2019 consolidation of
the rules says three, so the period was raised. The portal's
[OIDAR FAQ](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/registration/Non-Resident_Online_Services_Provider.htm)
adds the OIDAR specifics and no service level of its own. The application is processed at the
office of the Principal Commissioner of Central Tax, Bengaluru West. If the officer raises a
clarification, the applicant has seven working days to answer or the application may be
rejected. Part B has to be completed within fifteen days of Part A, and the certificate of
registration appears on the dashboard for printing rather than arriving by email. Whether rule
9's seven working days governs the OIDAR registration, which rule 14(2) leaves to "such officer
as may be notified", is not stated in either source and is left as a reading *(interpretation)*.

## 3. The window for registering

The ticket treats this as the most valuable answer, because it decides whether the trigger in
ticket 249 is late.

[Rule 10(2)](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/rules/cgst_rules/active/chapter3/rule10_v1.00.html)
states that the registration "shall be effective from the date on which the person becomes
liable to registration where the application for registration has been submitted within a period
of thirty days from such date". Rule 10(3) states that an application submitted after those
thirty days is effective only from the date of the grant of registration.

The portal's OIDAR FAQ states the same rule for this registration in the portal's own words
(FAQ 7). The effective date of registration on approval is the date of commencement of online
services in India if the difference between that date and the date of the application reference
number is thirty days or less, and otherwise the date of issue of the registration certificate.
Where the supplier was already providing the service before the appointed date, the later of the
commencement date and the appointed date is used.

So the trigger in ticket 249 is on time. Registering when a card sale's billing country is India
is late only if the application reference number is generated more than thirty days after the
date the online service began in India. Within those thirty days the registration is backdated
to the commencement date, and the tax for the supplies in the window is due because
[IGST section 14(1)](https://indiacode.gov.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/c34dadd9-0609-4cd5-89c5-2f6750daa70b/content)
makes the foreign supplier "the person liable for paying integrated tax" on the supply itself.
The thirty-day rule moves the effective date; it does not remove the tax on sales made before
the registration is granted *(interpretation)*.

The earlier note, `worldwide-obligations.md` section 2.2, said the rules as read state no lead
time before the first supply. That is corrected here: rule 10 supplies the window, and the
portal's OIDAR FAQ applies it to this registration.

One textual gap. Rule 10(1) lists the approvals that lead to a certificate of registration as
those under rules 9, 9A and 14A, and rule 14, the OIDAR registration rule, is not named. The
list was last amended by Notification 18/2025-CT with effect from 1 November 2025 (footnote to
rule 10(1)). Rules 10(2) and (3) are stated generally, and the portal's OIDAR FAQ applies the
thirty-day rule to OIDAR, which is the better evidence for this registration, but the rules'
own cross-reference does not name rule 14.

## 4. The rate and the nil return

The rate is 18 per cent.

The CBIC's "Rate of GST on Services", dated 3 July 2017, lists "All other services not
specified elsewhere" at 18 per cent in its [list of services at 18 per cent](https://www.cbic.gov.in/content/pdf/CONTENTREPO/gst/rrr/services-booklet-03July2017.pdf).
Online information and database access or retrieval services are not named in the booklet's nil,
5 per cent or 12 per cent lists, so they fall to the residuary entry. The booklet also carves
them out of the nil entry for services received from a supplier in a non-taxable territory,
which confirms they cannot take the nil rate. The instrument behind the schedule is Notification
No. 11/2017-Integrated Tax (Rate); the integrated tax rate notification itself could not be
fetched (section 6), so this finding rests on the CBIC booklet. The September 2025
rationalisation by the 56th GST Council changes named services such as passenger transport and
some job work to 5 per cent and does not name OIDAR, so 18 per cent stands
([CBIC FAQ, 56th meeting](https://www.cbic.gov.in/content/pdf/CONTENTREPO/gst/manuals_and_faqs/faq/FAQs_issued_56th_meeting_03092025.pdf)).

A nil return is due. [Rule 64](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/rules/cgst_rules/active/chapter8/rule64_v1.00.html)
requires every registered person providing OIDAR from a place outside India to a non-taxable
online recipient (and, since the substitution by Notification 51/2023-CT, supplies to a
registered person other than a non-taxable online recipient and online money gaming) to file
FORM GSTR-5A "on or before the twentieth day of the month succeeding the calendar month or part
thereof". The rule states no de minimis and no nil exemption, so a month with no Indian sales
still needs a nil GSTR-5A. The CBIC's general FAQ records the late filing fee of 100 rupees per
day under section 47 for a return not filed
([CBIC FAQ, 3rd edition](https://www.cbic.gov.in/content/pdf/CONTENTREPO/gst/manuals_and_faqs/faq/Final-GST-FAQ-31218.pdf)).

## 5. Whether the scheme obliges a person in India

[IGST section 14(2)](https://indiacode.gov.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/c34dadd9-0609-4cd5-89c5-2f6750daa70b/content),
as it stands in the consolidated Act dated 1 September 2026, reads:

> (2) The supplier of online information and database access or retrieval services referred to
> in sub-section (1) shall, for payment of integrated tax, take a single registration under the
> Simplified Registration Scheme to be notified by the Government:
>
> Provided that any person located in the taxable territory representing such supplier for any
> purpose in the taxable territory shall get registered and pay integrated tax on behalf of the
> supplier:
>
> Provided further that if such supplier does not have a physical presence or does not have a
> representative for any purpose in the taxable territory, he may appoint a person in the
> taxable territory for the purpose of paying integrated tax and such person shall be liable for
> payment of such tax.

The appointment is permitted, not imposed. The first proviso puts a duty on a representative
only if the supplier has one, and the second proviso applies where the supplier has neither a
physical presence nor a representative and says the supplier "may appoint" a person. The Act
does not require a UK sole trader to appoint anyone in India to take the registration. The
CBIC's [OIDAR flyer](https://www.cbic.gov.in/content/pdf/CONTENTREPO/gst/manuals_and_faqs/faq/OIDAR.pdf)
repeats the same three limbs: the representative, if there is one, registers and pays; and where
there is no physical presence or representative, the supplier may appoint a person.

## 7. What needs a lawyer or an accountant

- Which proof document a UK sole trader uploads for the third group, given there is no
  certificate of incorporation and possibly no origin-country licence.
- Whether the Indian authorised signatory is a legal necessity or a portal pre-requisite that
  can be satisfied by a nominee, and what liability that signatory carries.
- Whether registration must be complete before taking payment for a sale, given that the tax
  liability starts at the supply while the effective date can be backdated by thirty days.
- How an Indian authorised representative, if appointed, changes the UK sole trader's Indian
  permanent establishment and treaty position. This note does not examine that.
